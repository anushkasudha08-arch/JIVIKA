Nutrition & Diet

Good food does not have to be expensive or fancy. Simple meals from your local market can support your periods, bones, energy and mood.

Meal plans Budget foods

Food supports health — it does not replace prescribed treatment or medicines. If you take medicine for anaemia, thyroid, diabetes, blood pressure or any other condition, continue it exactly as prescribed. Never stop or change a dose because of a diet.

The simple plate

No weighing, no counting

  • Half the plate — vegetables and greens
  • One quarter — dal, egg, fish, paneer or chicken
  • One quarter — roti, rice or millet

Add a little oil or ghee, and drink water through the day. This plate works for most adults — portions differ in pregnancy, illness or heavy work.

Keep food safe

Prevents stomach infections

  • Wash vegetables and fruit with clean water before cutting.
  • Cook meat, fish and eggs fully; reheat leftovers only once.
  • Drink boiled, filtered or safely treated water where needed.
  • Do not keep cooked food at room temperature for more than 2 hours in hot weather.

The connection

How food supports your body

Food is not a cure, but it changes how you feel day to day. Here is what it can realistically do.

Menstrual health

Iron, vitamin C and omega-3

Every period loses iron. Regular iron-rich meals with vitamin C help replace it and reduce the tiredness that comes with heavy periods. Omega-3 fats from flax, walnuts and small fish may ease inflammation. Less salt and less strong tea can reduce bloating and cramps.

Bone health

Calcium, vitamin D and protein

Bone strength is built in the teens and protected after menopause. Ragi, sesame, curd and green leaves are cheap calcium sources. Vitamin D comes from morning sunlight as well as food. Walking, stairs and light strength work tell the bones to stay strong.

Steady energy

Iron, B12 and regular meals

Skipping meals causes the afternoon crash that many women blame on weakness. A protein source at each meal, whole grains instead of only refined flour, and enough water keep energy steadier. Constant tiredness needs a haemoglobin and B12 check — not another cup of tea.

Mood and gut

Fibre, curd and routine

The gut and the brain talk to each other. Fibre from vegetables and whole grains, and fermented foods such as curd, support gut health. Regular meal times help sleep and mood. Food can support wellbeing, but depression and anxiety need proper care — not a diet alone.

Affordable and local

Strong nutrition for little money

Breakfast

Poha with peanuts and lemon, or ragi porridge with milk

Stay safe

Supplements and when to ask for help

More is not better. Some supplements are useful when prescribed, and harmful when taken blindly.

Before buying a supplement

Iron, calcium, B12 and folic acid

  • Iron tablets should follow a haemoglobin test — too much iron damages the liver.
  • Calcium and iron tablets are best taken at different times of day.
  • Folic acid is started before pregnancy on a doctor's advice.
  • B12 supplementation matters for long-term vegetarian and vegan diets.
  • Do not buy “hormone balance”, “fat burner” or “slimming” products online without medical advice.

Ask your pharmacist to explain the dose, timing and side effects. Keep all medicines out of children's reach.

When food advice is not enough

Please see a doctor or dietitian

  • You cannot keep food or fluids down, or you are losing weight without trying.
  • Severe vomiting in pregnancy, or you feel dizzy and weak.
  • Constant tiredness with heavy periods, pale skin or breathlessness.
  • You have diabetes, kidney disease, thyroid problems or are pregnant — your diet needs individual advice.
  • A child is not growing or eating well — ask at the Anganwadi centre.
