Warning signs that need help now

These symptoms can become life-threatening within minutes or hours. Call an ambulance — do not wait for the next day, and do not travel alone.

Heart attack

Signs in women are often quieter

  • Chest pressure, tightness or heaviness lasting more than a few minutes
  • Breathlessness, cold sweat, nausea or unusual tiredness
  • Discomfort in the jaw, neck, back or one arm

Call 108. Sit down and stay calm. Do not drive yourself.

Stroke

Every minute counts

  • Face drooping on one side, or a crooked smile
  • One arm or leg suddenly weak or numb
  • Slurred speech, confusion or sudden loss of vision

Call 108 at once and note the time symptoms started.

Severe bleeding

Heavy periods included

  • Soaking one pad every hour for two hours or more
  • Bleeding after childbirth, an injury or a miscarriage
  • Dizziness, fainting or a racing heart with bleeding

Press firmly on a wound, lie down and call 108.

Breathing trouble

Or a severe allergic reaction

  • Cannot speak a full sentence or walk without gasping
  • Lips, face or fingertips turning blue
  • Swelling of lips or tongue after food, a sting or a medicine

Call 108. Sit upright and loosen tight clothing.

Pregnancy warning signs — go to hospital the same day: bleeding or fluid leaking, severe headache with blurred vision, swelling of the face or hands, fever, fits, or reduced baby movement.

Emergency care

What to do, what to say, who to call

A little preparation makes an emergency less frightening and gets help to you faster.

Calling 108 — what to say

Speak slowly and clearly; the operator will guide you

  1. 1

    Your location. Village or area name, a landmark, the road, and the house or shop number.

  2. 2

    What happened. The main symptom and when it started — for example “chest pain for 20 minutes”.

  3. 3

    Who needs help. Age, and whether she is pregnant or has just given birth.

  4. 4

    Condition right now. Conscious or not, breathing or not, bleeding or not.

  5. 5

    Stay on the line. Give your phone number and send someone to the road to guide the ambulance.

At the hospital

How to be heard and get the information you need

  • Carry an ID (Aadhaar), any health card, and medicine strips or a written list.
  • Describe symptoms in the order they started; mention pregnancy, allergies and long-term conditions.
  • Say clearly if you are in pain, bleeding heavily, or cannot breathe — do not wait quietly.
  • Ask what the diagnosis is, what each medicine is for, and when to come back.
  • Ask for a written prescription and a discharge note. Keep them for the next visit.
